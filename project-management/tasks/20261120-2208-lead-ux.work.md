### The `lead-ux` skill

**Layout.**

```
socle/agents/skills/lead-ux/
  SKILL.md                   the steps, each with its "Done when"
  SURVEY.md                  formats: survey.md and feedback.md
  EYE.md                     the eye's brief and fixed checklist
  scripts/capture.ts         the capture script (Deno, Playwright)
  templates/directions.html  the directions page template
```

`SKILL.md` links `SURVEY.md` and `EYE.md` as siblings
(`[SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md)`), read at the step that needs them: the
survey's format at step 2 and 5, the eye's brief at step 4.

**Frontmatter.** A chisel-owned skill: `name` and `description` only, no
`x-upstream`, no `disable-model-invocation`.

```yaml
---
name: lead-ux
description: Lead a UX research in short rounds before any code, when a task touches what a person sees or does — a new screen, a new flow, a redesign. Restates the ask, gathers real data and a survey, shows directions side by side, has a fresh eye check the captures, writes the human's feedback verbatim. Use at step 2 of `.agents/workflow.md`, or when the user asks what a screen should look like or wants UX directions.
---
```

The leading word is "Lead", first. One trigger per branch: step 2 of the
workflow, or the human's own ask.

**`SKILL.md`, outline.**

1. `# lead-ux — usage before technique`. Two sentences: the Architect runs
   it at step 2, before the spec leaves `draft`; it leaves three
   deliverables beside the spec, and those files are the memory: a round
   restarts from them, never from the session.
2. **The deliverables.** The tree and the table of the spec's "The three
   deliverables", word for word, under `<Tasks>/<task-id>/ux/`, where
   `<Tasks>` is the task workspace of `.agents/project.md` and `<task-id>`
   is the spec's file name without `.spec.md`. Names: *the survey*
   (`survey.md`), *the directions page* (`round-<n>/directions.html`),
   *the verbatim feedback* (`round-<n>/feedback.md`), plus `scripts/` and
   `data/`. Then what goes in git:
   - in: the survey, each feedback, each directions page, the generators,
     anonymised data, the captures a directions page shows
     (`round-<n>/captures/`);
   - out: a record that names a person or holds a client's data, in
     `ux/data/private/` with a `.gitignore` holding `*`; the eye's captures,
     in `round-<n>/eye/` with the same `.gitignore`. The skill writes those
     two `.gitignore` files itself and touches no other ignore file.
3. **Taste.** The skill carries none. Read the taste the project keeps —
   its design notes, if it has any — and every earlier `feedback.md`
   before drawing.
4. **The steps**, numbered, each ending on its **Done when** (below).
5. **When the project declares a screenshot command** in
   `.agents/project.md`, step 4 uses it in place of the script (the hook
   for relecture-humaine; see open question 4).

**The steps and their completion criteria.**

1. **Restate.** Five lines to the human: "I understand: … It is not: …".
   No research before the yes.
   *Done when:* the human said yes to a restatement; the spec's Context
   quotes the human's words as they were said, with a "What it is not"
   line; nothing exists under `ux/` yet.
2. **Real data and the survey.** Gather the real records the screen will
   show, first. Launch a background sub-agent with the `research` skill
   for prior art (`.agents/skills/research/SKILL.md`); its brief states
   the usage first. Write `survey.md` from
   [SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md).
   *Done when:* `ux/data/` holds the real records, personal ones under
   `ux/data/private/`; the research file is read; `survey.md` has its three
   parts — the data table, the audit block by block when the screen
   exists, the prior-art table — and is committed before any direction
   file exists.
3. **Directions.** Two to six that differ in structure, not in colour.
   Each named in two or three words, never a letter, with one line that
   sets it apart. Built by generators in `ux/scripts/`, on the real data,
   one command rebuilding every page. When the screen lives in a running
   app, build them on the real route with the UI branch of the
   `prototype` skill (`.agents/skills/prototype/UI.md`), then capture that
   route. The directions page starts from
   `.agents/skills/lead-ux/templates/directions.html`.
   *Done when:* one command, written at the top of `ux/scripts/`, rebuilds
   every direction and `round-<n>/directions.html`; every direction shows
   the real data; each has its name and its line; the page has a field
   under each direction, one for the whole page, and the copy button.
4. **The eye.** Run the capture script on the directions page, or on each
   route, into `round-<n>/eye/`. Read `report.md` — it is text. Launch a
   fresh sub-agent at the *mid* tier (`.agents/reference.md`, "Model
   tiers") with the brief of [EYE.md](./EYE.md). The agent that builds
   never opens a capture itself. Fix, re-capture, re-launch a fresh eye.
   *Done when:* the last `report.md` shows no overflow and no console
   error, or each one is carried to the human; the last eye's return marks
   every checklist item on every capture as clean, a defect with its
   position, or unverified; every defect is fixed or carried to the human.
5. **Show, then write the feedback.** Give the human the path of
   `round-<n>/directions.html`, with what the eye left unverified. The
   round stops there ("Where the work stops", `.agents/workflow.md`). The
   human's answer — the markdown the page's button copied, or their own
   words — goes into `round-<n>/feedback.md` per
   [SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md): verbatim, the agent's reading beside each
   word, then what the next round changes. Ask the human to correct the
   reading.
   *Done when:* `feedback.md` is committed, holds every word of the
   answer unedited, a reading beside each, and the list of changes for
   the next round.
6. **Next round or done.** A new round starts from the files — the survey,
   every `feedback.md`, the generators — and completes the survey first
   when the feedback taught something about the data or the usage.
   *Done when:* either a next round has started at step 3 from the files,
   or the human named one direction and the spec carries it — in Context
   the choice and its reasons in the human's words, in Architecture the
   structure chosen, both linking `ux/` — and the workflow resumes at
   step 2's seams.

**`SURVEY.md`, outline.**

- `# The survey and the feedback — formats`
- **`survey.md`**, three sections in this order:
  - *What the real data says*: a table `| What the data shows | What it
    changes |`, one row per finding, with the count or the example.
  - *The screen as it is* — only when the screen exists: its content block
    by block, a table `| Block | What it shows | Its source | The decision
    it serves |`, then four lists: doubles, what serves nothing, missing
    actions, the real journey step by step.
  - *What other products do*: a table `| Product | What we take | Why |`,
    each row citing the research file.
  - A last line per round: "Completed after round <n>: …".
- **`round-<n>/feedback.md`**:
  - a heading `# Round <n> — feedback`, the date, the path of the page
    shown;
  - one section per direction and one for the whole page, each a table
    `| The human's words | The reading |`, the words in quotes, unedited;
  - *What the next round changes*: a list, each item citing the words it
    comes from;
  - *Corrections of the reading*: filled when the human corrects one.

**`EYE.md`, outline.**

- `# The eye — brief and checklist`
- **Who.** A fresh sub-agent at the *mid* tier. It never saw the session;
  it reads images and returns text; it decides nothing and proposes no
  design.
- **The brief**, a block the Architect fills and sends as is:

  ```
  You check captures of UI directions. You did not build them.
  Captures: <round-n/eye/ path>, named <name>-<width>-<light|dark>.png.
  The script's text report: <round-n/eye/report.md>.
  Open every capture. For each one, go through the checklist below, item
  by item. Return a markdown table per capture:
  | Item | Clean / Defect / Unverified | Where (direction, area: top/middle/bottom, left/right) | What |
  Mark an item Unverified, with why, whenever the capture does not let you
  see it. Propose no fix and no design. Then list the report's findings
  you could not confirm on the images.
  ```
- **The checklist**, fixed, in this order:
  1. *Empty frames* — a frame, image, map or chart left blank or on a
     placeholder, often lazy loading that never fired.
  2. *Tiny images* — an image, avatar or icon too small to read what it
     shows.
  3. *Text crushed at 390 px* — words broken letter by letter, a column
     narrower than a word, text over text.
  4. *Low contrast* — text or a control that barely stands out from its
     background, in light or in dark.
  5. *A lone half-width element* — one block at half width with nothing
     beside it, where the row calls for a pair or the full width.
  6. *Overflow* — content cut at an edge, a horizontal scrollbar; checked
     against `report.md`.
- **What comes back to the builder**: the tables, never the images. An
  Unverified item goes to the human with the directions page.

**Writing pass.** `writing-great-skills` is `disable-model-invocation`, so
the Typist reads `.agents/skills/writing-great-skills/SKILL.md` and
`GLOSSARY.md` and passes `SKILL.md`, `SURVEY.md`, `EYE.md` and the
changed prose through it: front-loaded leading word, checkable "Done
when", no no-op, no negation where a positive says it, no duplication
between `SKILL.md` and its siblings. The worklog lists what the pass
changed (criterion **writing-checked**).

### The capture script

**Runtime.** Deno, in TypeScript: chisel needs Deno and nothing else
(README, 2.6+), so it is on every machine chisel equips. Playwright comes
through an `npm:` specifier pinned to one version, which pins the browser
revision with it:

```ts
import { chromium } from "npm:playwright@1.65.1";
```

The browser is installed once per machine by
`deno run -A npm:playwright@1.65.1 install chromium`; the script, when the
launch fails for a missing browser, prints that command and exits 1. The
installer copies the file without an exec bit, so it is always run through
`deno run`. Proven on this machine: Deno 2.9.8, Chrome Headless Shell
159.0.8154.6, a 390 px dark page with a 500 px element read
`scrollWidth 508` and saved a full-page capture.

**Command.**

```
deno run -A --no-config --no-lock .agents/skills/lead-ux/scripts/capture.ts <url-or-file> <out-dir> [--widths 390,1280] [--each <css>] [--name <prefix>]
```

`--no-config --no-lock` keep the run from touching the target repo's
`deno.json` or `deno.lock` (the probe of this plan added Playwright to this
repo's lock; that change was reverted).

- `<url-or-file>`: an `http(s)://` URL, or a path turned into a `file://`
  URL.
- `<out-dir>`: created if missing.
- `--widths`: default `390,1280`. Height 844 under 600 px wide, 800 above.
- `--each <css>`: also captures each matching element on its own, named by
  its `data-direction` attribute (slugged) or its index. The template marks
  each direction `[data-direction]`.
- `--name`: the file prefix; default, the file's base name or the URL's
  last path segment, else `page`.

**What it does, per width, per scheme (`light`, `dark`):** a new page with
that viewport and `colorScheme`; listen to `console` messages of type
`error` and to `pageerror`; `goto` with `waitUntil: "networkidle"`; scroll
to the bottom by one viewport at a time and back to the top, then wait for
`document.fonts.ready` and the network to idle again (lazy loading fires);
measure; `screenshot({ fullPage: true })`.

**Outputs.**

- `<out-dir>/<name>-<width>-<light|dark>.png`, four by default;
- with `--each`: `<out-dir>/<name>-<direction>-<width>-<light|dark>.png`;
- `<out-dir>/report.md`, one section per capture:
  - **Overflow**: `scrollWidth` against the viewport width, `none` or the
    excess in px, then up to ten elements whose right edge passes the
    viewport, each as `tag#id.class` and its width;
  - **Console errors**: each message, or `none`;
  - **Below the fold**: the headings, buttons, links styled as buttons,
    inputs and `[data-direction]` elements whose top is at or below the
    viewport height, each with its top in px; or `none`;
  - **Images**: those that failed to load (`naturalWidth` 0) and those
    rendered under 48 px wide, or `none`.
- Exit 0 when the captures are written, whatever the report says; exit 1
  on a missing browser or an unreachable page, with the reason on stderr.

**Shape**, a single file with no import but Playwright and Deno's own API:
`parseArgs` (from `Deno.args`, by hand, to keep one import), `toUrl`,
`captureOne(browser, url, width, scheme) → Section`, `renderReport(sections)
→ string`, a `main` that loops and writes. A header comment says what it
is, the browser install command, and that it is checked by a real run, not
by a suite.

### The directions page template

`templates/directions.html`: one file, no network, no build step, no
library; its CSS and script inline. It may reference the captures it shows
by a relative path (`captures/…`), which is why those captures are
committed.

- **Placeholders** a generator replaces: `{{title}}`, `{{round}}`, and a
  `<!-- DIRECTIONS -->` marker before which the generator writes one
  `<article class="direction" data-direction="<name>">` per direction. The
  template shows the article's markup in a comment: `<h2>` name, `<p
  class="line">` the line that sets it apart, `<div class="frame">` the
  direction itself (inline markup, an `<iframe srcdoc>` to isolate its
  styles, or an `<img>` of its capture), then `<label>Your words
  <textarea data-for="<name>"></textarea></label>`.
- **Side by side**: a CSS grid,
  `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(100%, 22rem), 1fr))`,
  so two to six directions sit side by side on a desktop and stack on a
  phone.
- **One field for the whole page**, after the grid: `<textarea
  data-for="page">`.
- **The copy button** builds markdown and copies it with
  `navigator.clipboard.writeText`; when that fails, it shows the markdown
  in a read-only textarea, selected, to copy by hand:

  ```markdown
  # Round <n> — <title>

  ## The whole page

  <text>

  ## <Direction name>

  <text>
  ```

  An empty field writes `(nothing)`. The fields are kept in
  `localStorage`, keyed by title and round, so a reload loses nothing.
- **Light and dark** through `prefers-color-scheme`, neutral system fonts,
  no brand: the page carries no one person's taste.

# The push waits for the local gates

## Goal

The human knows that "After the close" of `.agents/workflow.md` now says
the Architect pushes only once the gates of `.agents/project.md` pass
locally: CI confirms, it does not discover.

## Why

Agents pushed to let CI find what the local gates would have found,
loading the CI with runs that were bound to fail and making the wait for a
green build the slowest part of a task.

## Applies When

- The repo is updated from a version whose "After the close" in
  `.agents/workflow.md` says nothing of the gates before the push.

## Do Not Apply When

- Never: there is nothing to apply. `workflow.md` is a managed file and
  the copy `chisel update` makes carries the change; the session only
  reads it to the human.

## Observable Gaps

1. **What the human knows.** Signal: none in the repo. Say it to the
   human in one sentence: an agent pushes only once the gates of
   `.agents/project.md` pass locally; CI confirms, it is not where errors
   are found. A project whose gate section is empty runs what it has and
   says so.
   Done when: the human has read it.

## Out of Scope

- The CI configuration itself.

## Reference Paths

- `.agents/workflow.md` — **copy**, by the update.

## Validation

- Nothing to check in the repo: the closing update carries the change.

## Record Result

The closing `chisel update` records it, with the other informational

### Where it touches relecture-humaine

What this task leaves as hooks, by name, for the held task to plug into:

- **The three file names** — `ux/survey.md`, `ux/round-<n>/directions.html`,
  `ux/round-<n>/feedback.md` — fixed in `SKILL.md`. When relecture-humaine
  serves its pages, the directions page becomes one of them and the names
  stay.
- **The copy button's markdown** is the shape a served questions page can
  post back: one section per direction and one for the page.
- **The screenshot command**: `SKILL.md` says, generically, that a
  screenshot command declared in `.agents/project.md` takes the script's
  place. It names no section number and creates none (open question 4).
- **The survey's "before" captures**, when it audits an existing screen,
  sit in `ux/survey-captures/`, committed, where a later visual proof can
  take its "before". `SKILL.md` lists that folder in the deliverables tree.
- **Autoroute**: step 5 stops the round for the human, through the
  workflow's stop table; a task that runs `lead-ux` is therefore not small.

What it must not do: edit any file of `chisel-relecture-humaine`, create
§J or any section of `project.md.tpl`, create a `visual-proof` skill, serve
a page, or bring answers back other than through the conversation.
