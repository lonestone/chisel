### The `lead-ux` skill

**Layout.**

```
socle/agents/skills/lead-ux/
  SKILL.md                   size first, deliverables, who does what, the steps
  BRIEF.md                   the researcher's brief, and a next round's brief
  SURVEY.md                  formats: survey.md and feedback.md
  EYE.md                     the eye: cost bounds, brief, checklist
  scripts/capture.ts         the capture script (Deno, Playwright)
  templates/directions.html  the directions page template
```

`SKILL.md` links its siblings with sibling links (`[BRIEF.md](./BRIEF.md)`)
and reads each one at the step that needs it:

- `BRIEF.md` at steps 2 and 6;
- `SURVEY.md` at steps 1, 2 and 5;
- `EYE.md` at step 4.

The two files that are not markdown are cited by their installed path.
That is progressive disclosure along the branches: the quick round
never reads `BRIEF.md` or `SURVEY.md`.

**Frontmatter.** A chisel-owned skill: `name` and `description` only, no
`x-upstream`, no `disable-model-invocation`. It is model-invoked, because
step 2 of the workflow must reach it.

```yaml
---
name: lead-ux
description: Lead a UX research in short rounds before any code, when a task touches what a person sees or does — a new screen, a new flow, a redesign, a small request on a screen. Not for a light change — a colour, a label, a value. Use at step 2 of `.agents/workflow.md`, or when the user asks what a screen should look like or wants UX directions.
---
```

The leading word is "Lead", and it comes first. There is one trigger per
branch: step 2 of the workflow, or the human's own ask. Decision 15's
exclusion is in the description itself, so the model does not invoke the
skill on a light change. The old description listed the steps; that list
is cut, because the body already says it.

**`SKILL.md`, outline, in order.**

1. **`# lead-ux — usage before technique`.** Two sentences. The Architect
   runs it at step 2, before the spec leaves `draft`. It leaves its
   deliverables beside the spec, and those files are the memory: a round
   restarts from them, never from the session.

2. **Size first.** The Architect names the size in one line to the human,
   who can overrule it:
   - **A light change** — a colour, a label, a spacing, an icon, one
     value. No `lead-ux`: the spec gets its journey in one line.
   - **A small request** — one part of an existing screen laid out or used
     differently. This is a **quick round**:
     - no restating stop: one line, "I understand: …", heads the page;
     - no survey, no researcher, no round 0;
     - the Architect builds two or three proposals itself, on the real
       screen and its data, beside "As it is", one of them preferred;
     - it runs the capture script and reads its report, and the eye runs
       only when the report flags something;
     - then step 5.

     When the answer shows that the request is bigger, the next round is
     a full one.
   - **A new screen, a new flow, a redesign** — the full rounds, steps 1
     to 6.

   **In one shot** (`.agents/workflow.md`, "The one shot"), the skill
   runs when the task needs it and stops for no one (decision 16; see
   question 4 in the Notes). The restatement goes into the spec's Context
   without waiting. Round 0 is skipped, and *To keep* is filled from the
   audit, marked as the agent's reading. The preferred direction of the
   first round counts as chosen and is cited as step 6 says. The page
   stays for the human to read afterwards.

3. **The deliverables.** The spec's tree and table, word for word, under
   `<Tasks>/<task-id>/ux/`. `<Tasks>` is the task workspace of
   `.agents/project.md`, and `<task-id>` is the spec's file name without
   `.spec.md`. The names are *the survey* (`survey.md`), *the directions
   page* (`round-<n>/directions.html`) and *the verbatim feedback*
   (`round-<n>/feedback.md`), plus `scripts/` and `data/`. The tree adds
   the folders the steps use:
   - `round-0/`, the state of the place;
   - `survey-captures/`, the screen as it is, at scale 2;
   - `round-<n>/captures/`, what the page shows;
   - `round-<n>/eye/`, the eye's set;
   - `data/private/`.

   **What goes in git:**
   - in: the survey, each feedback, each directions page, the
     generators, the anonymised data, `survey-captures/` and
     `round-<n>/captures/`;
   - out: `data/private/`, a record that names a person or holds a
     client's data, and `round-<n>/eye/`. Each of those two folders holds
     a `.gitignore` with `*`. The skill writes those two files itself and
     touches no other ignore file.

4. **Who does what.**
   - **The Architect** runs steps 1, 4, 5 and 6 at its own tier.
   - **A researcher sub-agent** runs steps 2 and 3, at the Architect's
     tier, from [BRIEF.md](./BRIEF.md). It keeps the research out of the
     Architect's context. When its context grows, the drawing runs in a
     fresh session that reads the files.
   - **The eye** is a fresh sub-agent at *mid* (`.agents/reference.md`,
     "Model tiers"), launched from [EYE.md](./EYE.md).
   - The Architect sends fixes back to the researcher that built the
     directions, while it lives. When it is gone, a fresh one starts
     from the files and the next-round brief.

5. **Taste.** The skill carries none. Read the taste the project keeps,
   such as its design notes, and every earlier `feedback.md`, before
   drawing.

6. **The steps**, numbered, each ending on its **Done when** (below).

7. **The hook sentence** (Q4 and decision 14), generic, with no section
   number and no tool named: "When `.agents/project.md` declares a
   screenshot command, it replaces the capture script; when it declares
   how to show a page to the human, that replaces giving the path." See
   question 3 in the Notes for what main's §J does to the first half.

**The steps and their completion criteria.**

1. **Restate and frame — Architect.**
   - Five lines to the human: "I understand: … It is not: …".
   - With them:
     - the **scope**: which screens — these, the flow, the whole
       section;
     - the **kinds of question** the human will answer: a quick choice,
       a visual comparison, free text;
     - when it matters, the **usage**: the keyboard, the phone, touch,
       the mouse. Only a screen whose usage says so is built and checked
       for the keyboard.
   - A quote attributed to the human is checked at its source before it
     is written.
   - No research before the yes.
   - For an existing screen, **round 0** follows the yes. The Architect
     captures the screen as it is (`capture.ts`, scale 2, into
     `ux/survey-captures/`) and fills the template's round-0 form into
     `round-0/directions.html`. That page has one column, "As it is":
     - the captures;
     - its content block by block, as a table;
     - the usage problems, ranked blocking, major, minor;
     - the question *what must not move*.

     The page goes to the human as step 5 says, and the answer goes
     verbatim into `round-0/feedback.md`, per [SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md).

   *Done when:*
   - the human said yes to a restatement that names the scope, the kinds
     of question and, when it matters, the usage;
   - the spec's Context quotes the human's words as said, with a "What it
     is not" line;
   - for an existing screen, `round-0/feedback.md` is committed with the
     *To keep* answer;
   - no direction exists yet.

2. **Real data and the survey — researcher.**
   - The Architect launches the researcher with the first-round brief of
     [BRIEF.md](./BRIEF.md).
   - The researcher gathers the real records the screen will show, first,
     from a demo or an anonymised copy, never written to production.
   - It finds prior art the way `.agents/skills/research/SKILL.md` says.
   - It writes `survey.md` from [SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md), *To keep*
     carried over from round 0.

   *Done when:*
   - `ux/data/` holds the real records, the personal ones under
     `ux/data/private/`;
   - `survey.md` has every section that applies — the data table, the
     screen as it is, *Asked for or placed*, *To keep*, the prior-art
     table — and is committed before any direction file exists.

3. **Directions — researcher.**
   - Two to six directions that differ on named axes — the grouping, the
     density, how the supporting content shows, how one acts — never in
     colour alone.
   - Each is named in two or three words, never a letter. Under each go
     its idea, what it sacrifices, and "answers: …", the human's request
     it answers, or the mark "proposal" when no request names it.
   - *Asked for or placed* is re-read for each direction before it
     removes, lightens or moves anything.
   - For an existing screen, "As it is" is the first column.
   - One direction is **preferred**, with its reason in one line.
   - The page says what it shows and what it does not show yet.
   - The directions are built by generators in `ux/scripts/`, on the real
     data, and one command rebuilds every page.
   - When the screen lives in a running app, the directions are built on
     the real route with the UI branch of the `prototype` skill
     (`.agents/skills/prototype/UI.md`).
   - The page starts from
     `.agents/skills/lead-ux/templates/directions.html`.
   - The directions are keyboard-driven only when step 1's usage says so.

   *Done when:*
   - one command, written at the top of `ux/scripts/`, rebuilds every
     direction and `round-<n>/directions.html`;
   - every direction shows the real data and has its name, its idea, what
     it sacrifices, and its "answers:" line or its "proposal" mark;
   - exactly one direction is marked preferred, with its reason;
   - the page has a field under each direction, one for the whole page,
     and the copy button.

4. **The eye — Architect.**
   - The free check comes first: run the capture script with `--for-eye`
     and `--each "[data-direction]"`, adding `--keyboard` when the usage
     says so, into `round-<n>/eye/`, and read `report.md`, which is text.
   - Then one fresh eye at *mid* for every direction at once, briefed
     from [EYE.md](./EYE.md). In a quick round, the eye runs only when
     the report flags something.
   - The rule of `.agents/skills/visual-proof/SKILL.md`, "Looking: a fresh
     sub-agent", holds: the agent that builds never opens a capture.
   - Fixes go to the researcher. Then only the fixed directions are
     captured again (`--only`), and a fresh eye looks at those alone, once
     per round.

   *Done when:*
   - the last `report.md` shows no overflow, no console error and, with
     `--keyboard`, no control the Tab key misses — or each one is carried
     to the human;
   - the last eye marked every checklist item on every capture as clean,
     a defect with its position, or unverified;
   - every defect is fixed or carried to the human as unverified.

5. **Show, then write the feedback — Architect.**
   - Give the human the **absolute path** of `round-<n>/directions.html`,
     and open it when the session can: a browser tool of the host, or the
     system's `open` on the human's own machine.
   - Name the preferred direction and its reason.
   - Put the blocking questions first, each with its link into the page.
   - Say what the eye left unverified.
   - The round stops there ("Where the work stops", `.agents/workflow.md`),
     except in one shot.
   - The human's answer — the markdown the page's button copied, or their
     own words — goes into `round-<n>/feedback.md` per
     [SURVEY.md](./SURVEY.md): verbatim, the agent's reading beside each
     word, then what the next round changes.
   - Ask the human to correct the reading.

   *Done when:* `feedback.md` is committed and holds:
   - every word of the answer, unedited, with a reading beside each;
   - the list of changes for the next round.

6. **Next round or done — Architect.**
   - A new round starts from the files — the survey, every `feedback.md`,
     the generators — with the next-round brief of
     [BRIEF.md](./BRIEF.md).
   - It completes the survey first when the feedback taught something
     about the data or the usage.
   - It may merge what the human kept from several directions into two or
     three combinations.
   - It says in one line, on the page, whatever the human did not
     understand last time.
   - After the third round without a chosen direction, the Architect asks
     the human whether to go on, then again at each round after.
   - A choice that contradicts a decision already written in the spec is
     asked, not chosen.

   *Done when:* either a next round has started at step 3 from the files,
   or a direction is chosen — by the human, or, in one shot, the preferred
   one — and the spec carries it:
   - in Context, the choice and its reasons in the human's words;
   - in Architecture, the chosen direction cited by path: the
     `round-<n>/directions.html` path, the direction's name, its captures
     in `round-<n>/captures/`;
   - the workflow resumes at step 2's seams.

**`BRIEF.md`, outline.**

- `# The researcher's brief`
- One line on what it is for: the Architect fills it and sends it as it
  is; the researcher runs at the Architect's tier.
- **The first round**, a fenced block with placeholders, in the spec's
  order:
  1. *The human's intent*, quoted as said, complaints included, each
     quote checked at its source.
  2. *What I understand*, then *what it is not*: the neighbouring screens
     it must not become. Also the scope, the kinds of question (quick
     choice, visual comparison, free text) and the usage from step 1.
  3. *What you do — research, not code in the repo*:
     1. *The survey*: the kinds of product to look at, named — those that
        solve the same gesture, not the same domain — and what to note:
        what we take, and why. The method is
        `.agents/skills/research/SKILL.md`.
     2. *The house references*: what the human already liked in this
        project, with paths, and the taste the project keeps.
     3. *The real data*: where to take real records — a demo or an
        anonymised copy, never written to production, never invented —
        and the data model, with leave to propose a change, said apart.
     4. *Directions really different*: how many, on which axes, each a
        self-contained page filled with the real data, at phone and
        desktop width.
     5. *The directions page*, from the template, with under each
        direction its idea, what it sacrifices, its "answers:" line or
        its "proposal" mark, *Asked for or placed* re-read for it, and one
        direction preferred with its reason.
  4. *The project's rules* for mockups, when it keeps some.
  5. *Where the files go* (`<Tasks>/<task-id>/ux/`, the tree of
     `SKILL.md`), and *done*: the directions page written, its generator
     command at the top of `ux/scripts/`. The Architect runs the eye.
  6. *Write the survey to its file before drawing*: a long session loses
     what is not on disk. When the context grows, hand back after the
     survey, and the drawing runs in a fresh session.
- **A next round**, a short block:
  - the path of the round's `feedback.md`, to be read whole;
  - the gist in three lines;
  - what this round makes: the combinations, and the line for what was not
    understood.
- **What comes back to the Architect**: paths and a few lines, never
  images.

**`SURVEY.md`, outline.**

- `# The survey and the feedback — formats`
- **`survey.md`**, these sections in this order:
  - *What the real data says*: a table `| What the data shows | What it
    changes |`, one row per finding, with the count or the example.
  - *The screen as it is*, only when the screen exists:

  ```
  Captures: <round-n/eye/ path>, named <name>-<direction>-<width>-light.png.
  The script's text report: <round-n/eye/report.md>.

  by item. Return a markdown table per capture:
  | Item | Clean / Defect / Unverified | Where (direction, area: top/middle/bottom, left/right) | What |

  see it; never mark it absent. Change nothing, propose no fix and no
  design. Then list the report's findings you could not confirm on the
  images.
  ```
- **The checklist**, fixed, in this order. The keyboard is not an item:
  it is the script's `--keyboard` pass.
  1. *Empty frames*: a frame, image, map or chart left blank or on a
     placeholder, often lazy loading that never fired.
  2. *Tiny images*: an image, avatar or icon too small to read what it
     shows.
  3. *Text crushed at 390 px*: words broken letter by letter, a column
     narrower than a word, text over text.
  4. *Low contrast*: text or a control that barely stands out from its
     background.
  5. *A lone half-width element*: one block at half width with nothing
     beside it, where the row calls for a pair or the full width.
  6. *Overflow*: content cut at an edge, a horizontal scrollbar, checked
     against `report.md`.

### The capture script

**Runtime.** Deno, in TypeScript (Q1, the Owner: « Deno »). Chisel needs
Deno and nothing else, so Deno is on every machine chisel equips.
Playwright comes through an `npm:` specifier pinned to one version, which
pins the browser revision with it:

```ts
import { chromium } from "npm:playwright@1.65.1";
```

The browser is installed once per machine by
`deno run -A npm:playwright@1.65.1 install chromium`. When the launch
fails for a missing browser, the script prints that command and exits 1.
The installer copies the file without an exec bit, so it is always run
through `deno run`.

This is proven on this machine (2026-11-20): Deno 2.9.8, Chrome Headless
Shell 159.0.8154.6. A 390 px dark page with a 500 px element read
`scrollWidth 508` and saved a full-page capture.

It sits beside `visual-proof`'s Node `captures.ts`. That one compares
before → after on a running app, from the project's `node_modules`; this
one captures a local directions page, per direction, at scale 2, with a
keyboard pass, from Deno alone. See question 2 in the Notes.

**Command.**

```
deno run -A --no-config --no-lock .agents/skills/lead-ux/scripts/capture.ts <url-or-file> <out-dir> [--widths 390,1280] [--each <css>] [--only <name,name>] [--name <prefix>] [--for-eye] [--keyboard]
```

`--no-config --no-lock` keep the run from touching the target repo's
`deno.json` or `deno.lock`. The probe of this plan added Playwright to
this repo's lock, and that change was reverted.

- `<url-or-file>`: an `http(s)://` URL, or a path turned into a `file://`
  URL.
- `<out-dir>`: created if missing.
- `--widths`: default `390,1280`. The height is 844 under 600 px wide and
  800 above.
- **The page set, by default** (decisions 3 and 17): light and dark, at
  device scale factor 2. This is what `round-<n>/captures/` and
  `ux/survey-captures/` hold, for the human's page.
- `--for-eye`: light only, at device scale factor 1. This is the eye's
  set, into `round-<n>/eye/`.
- `--each <css>`: also captures each matching element on its own, named by
  its `data-direction` attribute (slugged) or else its index. The template
  marks each direction, "As it is" included, with `[data-direction]`.
- `--only <name,name>`: with `--each`, captures only those directions
  (decision 17: only the fixed directions again). The page-wide capture is
  skipped.
- `--name`: the file prefix. By default it is the file's base name or the
  URL's last path segment, else `page`.
- `--keyboard` (decision 18): the keyboard pass, off by default. Without
  it, no Tab is pressed and the report has no Keyboard section.

**What it does, per width and per scheme:**

1. open a new page with that viewport, `colorScheme` and
   `deviceScaleFactor`;
2. listen to `console` messages of type `error` and to `pageerror`;
3. `goto` with `waitUntil: "networkidle"`;
4. scroll to the bottom one viewport at a time and back to the top, then
   wait for `document.fonts.ready` and for the network to idle again, so
   lazy loading fires;
5. measure;
6. `screenshot({ fullPage: true })`, then each `--each` element with
   `locator.screenshot()`.

**The keyboard pass**, per width, light only, after the measures:

- An init script (`page.addInitScript`, which runs in every frame,
  `srcdoc` frames included) marks each element that receives `focusin`
  with `data-lead-ux-focused`.
- The script presses Tab until focus comes back to the first element it
  focused, or until it has pressed twice as many times as there are
  candidates, plus one.
- It lists the **visible controls never focused**. A candidate is any
  visible element matching `a[href], button, input, select, textarea,
  summary, [tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"]), [contenteditable]`, plus any
  element with `role="button"` or an `onclick` attribute, in every frame.
  Visible means a non-empty bounding box and not `visibility: hidden`.
- Each is written as `tag#id.class` with its direction.

Whether `addInitScript` reaches `srcdoc` frames is checked by the real run
(criterion **keyboard-pass-runs**). If it does not, the pass also injects
the listener with `frame.evaluate` after load, and the worklog says so.

**Outputs.**

- `<out-dir>/<name>-<width>-<light|dark>.png`: four for the page set, two
  for `--for-eye`;
- with `--each`: `<out-dir>/<name>-<direction>-<width>-<light|dark>.png`;
- `<out-dir>/report.md`, a head line with the URL, the scale and the
  schemes, then one section per capture:
  - **Overflow**: `scrollWidth` against the viewport width, `none` or the
    excess in px, then up to ten elements whose right edge passes the
    viewport, each as `tag#id.class` with its width;
  - **Console errors**: each message, or `none`;
  - **Below the fold**: the headings, buttons, links styled as buttons,
    inputs and `[data-direction]` elements whose top is at or below the
    viewport height, each with its top in px, or `none`;
  - **Images**: those that failed to load (`naturalWidth` 0) and those
    rendered under 48 px wide, or `none`;
  - **Keyboard**, with `--keyboard` only: the controls never focused, or
    `none`.
- Exit 0 when the captures are written, whatever the report says. Exit 1
  on a missing browser, an unreachable page, or `--only` without `--each`,
  with the reason on stderr.

**Shape.** A single file with no import but Playwright and Deno's own API:

- `parseArgs`, written by hand from `Deno.args`, to keep one import;
- `toUrl`;
- `captureOne(browser, url, width, scheme, scale, opts) → Section`;
- `keyboardPass(page) → string[]`;
- `renderReport(head, sections) → string`;
- a `main` that loops and writes.

A header comment says:

- what the script is;
- the browser install command;
- that a repo whose own checker scans every `.ts` excludes `.agents/`;
- that it is checked by a real run, not by the suite.

The script's names and its report stay in English, as in the rest of the
socle; `visual-proof`'s French report is that script's own.

### The directions page template

`templates/directions.html` is one file, with no network, no build step
and no library; its CSS and script are inline (decision 14). It may
reference the captures it shows by relative path (`captures/…`), which is
why those captures are committed.

**Placeholders and markers** a generator replaces:

- `{{title}}` and `{{round}}`;
- `{{understanding}}`, the "I understand: …" line that heads a quick
  round;
- `{{shows}}`, what the page shows and what it does not show yet
  (decision 11);
- a `<!-- QUESTIONS -->` marker: the questions to the human, blocking
  first, each with a link to its direction's anchor (`#d-<slug>`);
- a `<!-- DIRECTIONS -->` marker. Before it, the generator writes one
  `<article class="direction" id="d-<slug>" data-direction="<name>">`
  per direction. A comment in the template shows the article's markup:
  - `<h2>`, the name;
  - `<p class="idea">` and `<p class="sacrifices">`;
  - `<p class="answers">`, "answers: …" or "proposal — no request names
    it yet";
  - on the preferred one only, `data-preferred` on the article and
    `<p class="preferred">Preferred: <reason></p>`;
  - `<div class="frame">`, the direction itself: inline markup, an
    `<iframe srcdoc>` to isolate its styles, or an `<img>` of its
    capture;
  - `<label>Your words <textarea data-for="<name>"></textarea></label>`.

**Layout.**

- **"As it is" comes first** for an existing screen (decision 9):
  `data-direction="as-it-is"` and class `as-it-is`, shown as the
  reference, with no preferred mark and no "answers" line.
- **Round 0** is the same template, with `{{round}}` set to `0`. It has
  only the "As it is" article, whose frame holds the captures and the
  audit tables, and a field `data-for="to-keep"` labelled *What must not
  move*.
- **Side by side**: a CSS grid,
  `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(100%, 22rem), 1fr))`.
  Two to six directions sit side by side on a desktop and stack on a
  phone.
- **One field for the whole page** comes after the grid:
  `<textarea data-for="page">`.

**The copy button** builds markdown and copies it with
`navigator.clipboard.writeText`. When that fails, it shows the markdown
in a read-only textarea, selected, to copy by hand:

```markdown
# Round <n> — <title>

## The whole page

<text>

## <Direction name> (preferred)

<text>

## <Direction name>

<text>
```

- Round 0 writes `## What must not move` in place of the direction
  sections.
- An empty field writes `(nothing)`.
- The fields are kept in `localStorage`, keyed by title and round, so a
  reload loses nothing.

**A guard the capture report sees** (decision 16): on load, when the
round is not `0` and the page has not exactly one `[data-preferred]`
article, the script writes
`console.error("lead-ux: mark exactly one preferred direction")`. The
capture report's console errors then show a page that forgot its
preferred direction.

**Controls** (decision 18): the page's own fields and copy button are
plain `<textarea>` and `<button>`, which a keyboard reaches at no cost.

**Look**: light and dark through `prefers-color-scheme`, neutral system
fonts, no brand. The page carries no one person's taste.
